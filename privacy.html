This policy covers GlassPad for iPhone and iPad, GlassPad Mac, and this website. GlassPad is developed by Ashutosh Wahane.

App data and local connections

GlassPad does not require an app account and does not include advertising. Optional usage analytics is described below and is off unless you turn it on. We do not operate a server that receives your pointer movements, keystrokes, typed text, or pairing codes.

The app sends input commands and text you choose to send directly to your paired Mac using an encrypted local network connection. The receiving Mac passes that input to its active application. Those applications may store what you enter under their own privacy policies.

Pairing details and connection state are kept in memory while the apps run. Nearby Mac names are discovered on your local network. Preferences such as pointer speed, scrolling, theme, and whether a successful Mac connection has occurred are stored locally on your device. This connection preference only enables the trial activation button; it never grants access or stores the trial start date. Quitting ends the in-memory session; a fresh pairing is required after quitting.

Launcher and Mac widgets

When connected, the Mac companion sends your iPhone or iPad the names and icons of apps on your Mac so you can add them to the Launcher. Your Launcher pages, tiles, websites and Shortcut names are stored locally on your iPhone or iPad. Icons received from the Mac are kept in memory and cleared when you disconnect.

Mac widgets show which supported app is in front on your Mac and, for Music or Spotify, what is playing (track title, artist, artwork and playback position), and let you control it. This information travels only over the same encrypted local connection and is not sent to us.

Launcher actions open the apps, websites and Shortcuts you choose on your Mac. Window actions are performed by macOS on your Mac.

Optional usage analytics

GlassPad for iPhone and iPad includes Google Analytics for Firebase, provided by Google. It is off by default and only starts if you turn on Settings → Share usage analytics. GlassPad Mac and this website do not include it.

When turned on, GlassPad sends Google:

  • an app-instance identifier created by Firebase (not your advertising identifier or vendor identifier),
  • app sessions and basic device and app information collected by the Firebase SDK, such as device model, OS version, app version and approximate country derived from the connection,
  • that a Mac connection succeeded and that a remote-control session started,
  • completed lifetime purchases (product and price), as reported by Apple’s StoreKit.

GlassPad never sends your typed text, keystrokes, pointer movements, pairing codes, Mac names, Mac app names, Launcher contents, or what is playing on your Mac. Advertising features, ad personalisation and the advertising identifier are disabled. Analytics data is not used to track you across other companies’ apps or websites and is not sold.

We use these reports to understand how many sessions connect successfully and how purchases perform. Google processes this data under the Google Privacy Policy and the Firebase data processing terms. Reports are retained according to the Google Analytics retention setting for the project.

You can turn sharing off at any time in the same setting. Turning it off stops collection and deletes analytics data stored on your device that has not yet been sent. To ask about data already sent, email us (below).

Permissions

Local Network access lets the apps discover and connect to each other. Camera access scans the Mac pairing QR code on your device; GlassPad does not save or upload camera images. You can paste a pairing code instead. Pasting is initiated by you. Mac Accessibility permission allows the companion to deliver mouse and keyboard input and move, resize or minimise windows when you use window actions. Mac Automation permission, if you allow it, lets the companion read song information from and control Music or Spotify for widgets.

You can revoke these permissions in device Settings or macOS System Settings. Disconnect or pause the companion to stop control. Removing the app removes its local app data, subject to your device backup and operating system behavior.

Purchases

Apple processes purchases. GlassPad reads verified StoreKit transaction information to determine whether your lifetime unlock or trial is active, including purchase dates needed to calculate trial expiry. We do not receive your payment card details. Apple’s own privacy policy applies to its billing services. Restoring purchases uses Apple’s purchase history. The seven-day full-app trial starts with the verified free trial transaction after successful pairing. Reinstalling or restoring on another device does not restart its original dates. Access diagnostics display the verified entitlement state and StoreKit environment locally; they are not sent to us. If you have turned on usage analytics, a completed lifetime purchase is reported as described above.

Website and support

This website has no analytics scripts or advertising. It is hosted by GitHub Pages; GitHub may process technical request information, such as IP addresses, to operate its service under GitHub’s privacy statement.

If you contact us through GitHub Issues, your message and GitHub profile are public. We use that information to answer questions and investigate problems. Do not post sensitive information. You can edit or delete your own issue content subject to GitHub’s controls and retention policies.

For email support, we use your email address and the information you provide to respond and resolve the request. Support emails are handled through Gmail and retained while needed for support and related records. You may request deletion by email, subject to any necessary legal retention.

Children

GlassPad is not directed to children and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
